> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

Localize review instructions but omit proof for high-severity claims and zero-finding acceptance. Matching headings/terms can still change behavior; frozen language versions contain such differences.

## Mechanism

Extract controls for permission, evidence, stop/escalation, thresholds and output fields with locators. Map translated items as retained/omitted/weakened/strengthened and test common conditions. Record canonical contradictions separately instead of treating source as infallible. Correct material differences or document accepted changes, then independently check identifiers/runtime behavior.

## Bad example

```text
Equivalent terms/headings prove parity, even without high-severity evidence or with compulsory invented findings.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Build control mappings for evidence gates, zero findings, unavailable-tool fallback, stops and fields. Repair omissions and mark conflicting source policies for ruling. Natural wording may differ without changing conditions/outcomes.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Operational semantics require conditions/actions, not nouns alone. A control table exposes hidden strengthening/weakening of authority/evidence/acceptance.

## Observable expectation

Teaching unsupported high-severity claims downgrade/remain pending under the contract; clean reviews may have zero findings. Unavailable browsers are static review, not live validation. Equivalent inputs should face equal gates; document comparisons are not model efficacy runs.

## Limits

Frozen English/Japanese sources differ in thresholds/features and are not current product facts. Parity does not establish runtime enforcement; identifiers/schema need separate checks. Authorized policy changes must be labeled changes, not equivalent translation.
