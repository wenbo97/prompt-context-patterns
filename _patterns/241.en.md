> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

A release entrypoint needs code/security reports. Teaching leaf roles inspect domains, workflows supply steps and the entrypoint owns collection/decision scope. A single security question does not require nested coordinator personas.

## Mechanism

Separate process, perspective and entrypoint using minimal routing. Directly invoke one lens; fan out independent lenses and collect/verify/synthesize centrally. Avoid layers repeating the same task. Reports do not independently trigger publication.

## Bad example

```text
Each checker creates another coordinator, repeatedly paraphrases/delegates and assumes someone else collects; every role may decide to publish.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
The teaching entrypoint gets two leaf reports at one revision/input/format. Roles use relevant checking workflows without redundant coordination layers. The entrypoint collects/verifies with disagreement and actual state retained. Publication follows its separate scope; single-lens work is direct with explicit ownership.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Clear responsibilities converge on an entrypoint with full task scope. Avoiding useless relays preserves identity and prevents ambiguous collection ownership.

## Observable expectation

Illustrative flow is entrypoint→code/security→entrypoint verification. One owner synthesizes, and missing output stays incomplete. Inspect no leaf publishing or recursive relay.

Two roles existing proves no executed review.

## Limits

Flat reports fit known artifacts; causal investigation may need interaction. Source persona restrictions are workflow choices, not a universal ban on useful hierarchy. Every coordination layer needs a real responsibility.
