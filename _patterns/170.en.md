> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

While editing a process explanation, you find approval→remote login and local setup→local build as independent paths. A small diagram may clarify dependency/parallelism, unlike offering every visualization tool before reading.

## Mechanism

Do necessary work first, then offer optional capability tied to an observed communication gap and reader benefit. A dependency diagram is optional, not a prerequisite for independent text edits. Continue text while unanswered and add the chosen enhancement within scope. Never classify required permission/accessibility as optional.

## Bad example

```text
Before reading, ask the user to choose all visualization, animation and export tools, stopping every edit until answered.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Revise the supplied process text first. Offer a small dependency graph because the two paths can be mistaken for one fixed sequence; explain its benefit. Keep it optional and continue text if unselected without repeated promotion. Follow explicit existing choices and preserve required authorization as required.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Observed need makes an offer assessable. Separating optional/required decisions reduces tool-menu burden without blocking settled work on unanswered preferences.

## Observable expectation

The illustrative offer identifies the dependency section and independence of paths. Unanswered choice still permits text completion; declining a graph keeps the text solution.

Reject unrelated effects/exports and required access decisions bypassed by defaults.

## Limits

Explicit visualization requests can proceed directly without artificial questions. Verify actual capability availability. Provenance remains unconfirmed, and an optional offer grants no external-service/paid-call permission.
