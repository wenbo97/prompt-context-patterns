> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

Coauthor a migration guide with confirmed compatibility but unresolved rollout. Teaching facts say old clients remain supported during transition and an adaptation layer exists; date/rollback criteria are absent. Draft known sections without hiding gaps in a complete-looking document.

## Mechanism

Agree audience, outline and section objective; draft one reviewable unit from facts. Incorporate consequential feedback and record settled/open points before extension. Leave named rollout questions while independent compatibility work proceeds. Read the whole document for cross-section consistency instead of mechanically repeating approval gates.

## Bad example

```text
Write the entire guide immediately, guessing rollout date and rollback conditions alongside confirmed compatibility, then request one final review.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Draft compatibility from the supplied old-client/adaptation facts, show it and record corrections. Keep rollout date/rollback criteria open until its owner provides them, continuing independent content. Preserve confirmed decisions/provenance and inspect whole-document flow. Do not invent steps or make section collaboration a mandatory reapproval for every edit.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

A coherent section focuses feedback while missing facts stay visible without blocking other material. Whole-document review then reconciles local choices that might otherwise conflict.

## Observable expectation

The fixture has a factual compatibility section and open rollout items without invented dates. Changed old-client conditions update that section and related references.

Trace completed sections to confirmed facts and keep unanswered feedback distinct from acceptance.

## Limits

Settled requirements can support a full draft without artificial questions. Source fixed counts are workflow choices; adapt to real gaps. Teaching rollout prose grants no release permission.
