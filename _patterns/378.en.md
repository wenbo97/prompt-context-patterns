> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

Baseline and overlay both complete three teaching behaviors. ON success shows task capability, not added benefit; tools/cost can still regress.

## Mechanism

Separate behavioral acceptance from matched ON/OFF evaluation with fixed cases/runtime/budget/definitions and raw rows. Verify intervention engagement and compute predefined quality/resource effects, labeling saturation, no detected gain, regression or incompleteness. Detector validity is also distinct from benefit of acting on signals, which requires outcome controls.

## Bad example

```text
ON solved all, so claim 20% improvement without OFF and hide higher resource use inside pass.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Test OFF/ON on the same fixture, accept behaviors separately and compare complete rows. Both 3/3 means baseline_saturated and no demonstrated marginal correctness benefit; retain resource changes. Missing comparisons are incomplete, not zeros.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Task success and intervention benefit differ. Controls and discriminating metrics measure marginal effect; single-arm success credits preexisting capability to the change.

## Observable expectation

Teaching OFF 3/3 and ON 3/3 yield delta 0; higher ON calls are a separate regression. Accurate detection without action-outcome comparison cannot establish useful intervention. These are virtual checks; free harness tests are not model efficacy.

## Limits

Saturated fixtures lack discrimination, not proof of universal uselessness. Control state/sampling and define percentages, zero baselines and cost basis. Frozen 20% examples are not universal promises.
