> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

Choose speech output from an accessible potentially paged catalog. Teaching M1 lacks TTS, M2 supports it and voice-v1 is listed. IDs/fields are synthetic rather than current product names.

## Mechanism

Inspect current capabilities/schema/paging, filter required features/formats/parameters and select returned identifiers/voice. Handle removal/access changes through rediscovery rather than invented substitutes. Catalog and actual output compatibility require separate evidence.

## Bad example

```text
Use remembered IDs, assume TTS on every model, read one page and guess missing voices.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Read the teaching complete accessible catalog, verify M2 TTS/format/parameters and listed voice-v1, rejecting M1. Use returned IDs. Missing pages/metadata remain incomplete; removal/access errors trigger inspection/rediscovery, with no suitable option an explicit gap. Do not invent alternatives.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Current metadata binds identity to capability, separating past existence, access and speech support. Coverage/change handling prevents first-page absence becoming universal absence.

## Observable expectation

The fixture chooses evidenced M2/voice-v1; removed voice does not remain valid, and incomplete catalogs retain scope. Actual output probes need authorized execution.

No speech is generated or frozen model/limit promoted to current fact.

## Limits

Metadata expires and establishes neither quality/cost nor authorization. Provider shapes differ; parse actual schemas rather than assumed nested structure.
