> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

One private account repeats across report/trace, while distinct accounts may collapse into REDACTED and lose linkage. Teaching substitutions operate on export copies only.

## Mechanism

Use policy-approved structural mappings: same identity across files, distinct identities separated. Preserve safe line/event markers and resolve redacted-key collisions with opaque IDs/stable suffixes. Redact before encoding/truncation. Keep original maps out of export/logs; log placeholder/category/count only. Recount final non-log files and inspect evidence usefulness.

## Bad example

```text
Use ACCOUNT-1 and REDACTED for one account, collapse all path keys and ship the original map.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Use ORG-1 consistently and ORG-2 for another. Preserve safe markers, suffix colliding keys and redact before serialization/truncation. Logs contain placeholders/counts only; record evidence limitations.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Stable substitutions preserve relationships and distinct observations. Structural preprocessing avoids truncation leaks/key loss; excluded maps reduce reidentification.

## Observable expectation

Teaching same-identity labels match and distinct labels differ. Colliding keys retain both entries, counts match final files and no original map ships. Mark redactions inside quotes rather than implying original wording.

## Limits

Markers/placeholders can still identify indirectly under real policies. Stable mappings may be export-local, not permission for cross-dataset tracking. Disclose lost support rather than retaining secrets.
