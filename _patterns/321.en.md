> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

Explore file-import interfaces for one shared read/validate/result requirement. Teaching constraints differ: few entrypoints, format extensibility and easy common calls. Best-interface alone can produce near-identical proposals.

## Mechanism

Vary optimization constraints while sharing domain terms/facts and output contract: interface, usage, hidden implementation, dependencies and tradeoffs. Compare actual behavior/shape, not names, and justify hybrids. One designer can explore sequentially; agent count is not diversity evidence.

## Bad example

```text
Request three best interfaces without different goals, then choose by naming/votes near the first idea.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Explore teaching A minimal entrypoints, B new-format extension and C easy single-file defaults. Require exact interfaces/invariants, usage, hidden work, dependencies and costs. Compare caller knowledge, extension and errors, not renamed functions. Explain recommendation/hybrid fit without assuming natural independence.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Constraints expose different tradeoffs; common facts/formats make comparison valid. Diversity comes from actual interface consequences, not repeated identical prompts.

## Observable expectation

An illustrative comparison distinguishes unified entry, registration and convenient defaults. Three renamed identical functions fail.

All retain shared validation/errors; omitted boundaries cannot manufacture flexibility. No real API is built.

## Limits

Brief independence guarantees no thought independence. Counts/entry limits are source strategies rather than universal optima, and differentiated objectives cannot override common requirements.
