> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

Three planned teaching trials pass twice then make a forbidden write. Continuing until three passes cannot turn the original experiment green.

## Mechanism

Predeclare IDs, count, variation tolerance, nonwaivable contracts and missing/error rules. Retain all attempts/resources and judge as registered; majority cannot override contract violation. Repairs get new experiment identities while failures remain. Define infrastructure retries beforehand rather than after outcomes.

## Bad example

```text
Replace one failure by sampling until three successes and report 100%.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Keep two passes and a forbidden write as a contract failure. Use declared majority rules only for acceptable wording variation; missing trials mean incomplete. Retest repairs as a new experiment, preserving failed rows and thresholds.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Fixed panels protect denominators/conclusions from favorable replacement. Separating contracts from acceptable randomness prevents majority exemption for explicit violations.

## Observable expectation

Teaching[P,P,contract-fail]fails;[P,P,missing]is incomplete;[P,P,wording-fail]passes only under predeclared 2/3 acceptance and reports 2/3. Reconcile counts/resources with registration.

## Limits

Three trials/majority are illustrative, not population reliability. Sample size, infrastructure handling and independence are task-specific; source paid-no-retry rules are not universal. No model evaluation ran here.
