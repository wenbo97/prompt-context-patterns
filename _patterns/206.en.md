> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

Choose durable storage with future maintainers in mind. Teaching A is current-state tables plus audit log; B is appended events plus derived current view. Requirements preserve history and read current state often. Reversing B may require data/consumer migration rather than a trivial rename.

## Mechanism

Apply three ADR heuristics: meaningful reversal cost, surprising without context and genuine alternatives/tradeoffs. When all hold, record context, options, reasons, consequences and revisit conditions. Routine conventions belong in ordinary docs, while mandatory organizational records follow their own rules.

## Bad example

```text
Write an ADR for every variable rename but describe storage B only as more advanced, omitting A, query cost and migration context.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Check A/B against reversal cost from migrations, contextual surprise and genuine history/query tradeoffs. If satisfied, record the B decision: history requirement, alternatives, centralized event rationale, derived-query/consistency costs and review conditions. Use ordinary change notes for routine renames; do not claim B universally superior.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Decision records preserve consequential context rather than increasing document counts. Alternatives/consequences let maintainers reassess instead of inheriting an unsupported advanced label.

## Observable expectation

An illustrative ADR explains why not A, benefits/costs of B and changes requiring review. 'B is better' fails; a rename usually lacks the three conditions.

Ground reasons in supplied requirements/comparisons and preserve unknown costs instead of invented performance.

## Limits

These are documentation heuristics, not legal/compliance criteria or a storage recommendation formula. Real organizations may differ. An ADR neither proves correctness nor authorizes production migration.
