> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

After adding import-failure logs/alerts, verify collection, aggregation, lookup and test notification. Teaching test-r9 creates a controlled failure in isolation; logger code alone does not establish a working chain.

## Mechanism

Within authorized test scope, generate a unique signal with time/environment. Check structured local fields, aggregator requestId lookup, cross-service traces, metric labels, test receipt and runbook link. Bound ingestion waits and locate missing stages. Use approved test channels and reversible settings, restoring thresholds afterward with scope recorded.

## Bad example

```text
Logger calls exist, so monitoring is ready. Missing events mean no failure; ignore collection and alerts.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Trigger isolated test-r9 import failure and inspect structured logs, aggregation, trace and test alerts by ID. Record found/missing stages and bounded waits, checking destination/runbook. Without execution mark unverified; do not send production alerts.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Telemetry can fail too. A known input tests the channel, and ID tracing separates absent business activity from lost evidence. Configuration presence does not replace observing outputs.

## Observable expectation

Local test-r9 with no aggregate record reveals collection failure, not no error. Wrong-channel notification fails too. A passing teaching record binds event/trace/metric/test receipt to one identity and confirms restored thresholds. This article fires no actual alerts.

## Limits

One signal does not establish load or long-term reliability; sampling/redaction changes evidence. Induced errors and notifications follow actual authorization. Disclose unsupported evidence types and restore temporary thresholds; production checks remain separate.
