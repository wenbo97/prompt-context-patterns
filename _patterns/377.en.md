> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

Design review R1 accepted keyboard access and visible focus; engineering review R2 changes loading. Update the current plan without retaining only the latest reviewer's suggestions. Teaching obligations are tied to their accepted revision.

## Mechanism

Separate editable implementation plans from review history. Track accepted requirements with source, conditions and checks. Carry forward still-valid obligations and record additions/replacements under R2. Expose conflicts and explicitly superseding user decisions rather than editing history to erase earlier requirements.

## Bad example

```text
Rewrite for engineering, deleting R1 keyboard/focus requirements. Keep only R2 loading advice and change R1 history to say no obligations were accepted.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Revise the current plan while retaining R1 keyboard access, visible focus and verification conditions. R2 records loading changes/evidence without rewriting R1. If loading conflicts with accessibility, identify it and a proposed resolution; record authorized supersession after a valid decision. Do not silently remove obligations through rewriting or compaction.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Latest-only rewriting loses accepted commitments and obscures attribution. Current-plan references plus preserved history let recipients inspect valid conditions and actual amendments.

## Observable expectation

An illustrative R2 plan contains keyboard/focus plus loading changes, while R1/R2 histories preserve separate decisions. Later explicit changes cite the superseded requirement and source.

Reject invented R1 None records, dropped checks or expansion of historical approval into new external permission.

## Limits

Explicit later decisions can replace obligations; history is not timeless authorization. Source automation modes, phase labels and tooling are not prerequisites. Preserve actual provenance, conditions and amendment relationships.
