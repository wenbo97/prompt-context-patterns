> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

A candidate scores 0.8 this week against last week’s0.7 baseline after serving conditions changed. Rerun the unchanged prompt now to distinguish drift from intervention.

## Mechanism

Record code, lockfile, patches, tools, model/configuration fingerprints and historical differences. Repeat control and candidate under current matched cases/cache/state; rebuild stochastic intermediates rather than only rescore old artifacts. Retain historical records and gate on contemporary control with errors/variation. Control runs follow approved budgets.

## Bad example

```text
Attribute this week’s C0.8 minus last week’s B0.7 entirely to the prompt edit without environment checks.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Run unchanged B and C in the current environment with matched cases/state. Record fingerprints, identities, repetitions and failures. If B also becomes 0.8, C cannot claim a0.1 gain. Preserve historical 0.7 with its old conditions.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

No-change controls measure environmental movement for the same system so candidates do not inherit that credit. Identity and matched conditions prevent historical numbers becoming permanent standards.

## Observable expectation

Teaching historical B0.7 and current B0.8/C0.8 show no distinguishable contemporary difference. Current B0.7/C0.8 still needs replication/noise checks. Confirm unchanged prompts and newly built stochastic controls. Values are illustrative.

## Limits

Fingerprints miss hidden provider changes, and cache/state can confound. Frozen-output blind references differ from contemporary system controls and need separate metrics. Controls alone do not establish causation/general benefit; large temporal gaps warrant design reassessment.
