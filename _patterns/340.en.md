> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

Capture run-a finalizes after stopping; its artifact event can arrive later. Teaching logs also contain old run-b exported. Wait for run-a's own terminal state without killing observation early or accepting stale evidence.

## Mechanism

Stop the owned producer under protocol while retaining observation. Within a deadline filter current-run completion/failure and record artifact identity/state. After result or explicit unresolved timeout, clean owned observation; reconcile disconnect/uncertain outcomes through status rather than duplicate capture.

## Bad example

```text
Kill the listener first, stop run-a and accept run-b exported as current completion without run-a evidence/artifact identity.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Stop teaching run-a while retaining its observer. Await matching run-a exported/failed, ignoring run-b. Record terminal/artifact identity then clean owned observation. Timeout remains unconfirmed with status reconciliation/cleanup handling, not blind restart or completion. Verify current process ownership.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Producer shutdown can begin export rather than finish it. Observation plus run identity distinguish stop/completion/stale logs and define cleanup timing.

## Observable expectation

Stop run-a→run-b exported→run-a exported confirms only the last event. Failed/timeout yield failure/unconfirmed states.

Inspect observer survival and stale-event rejection; receipts do not establish quality.

## Limits

Events/shutdown/reconciliation are service-specific; frozen PID/path commands are not current host values. Receipts promise neither durability nor quality, and cleanup targets owned resources only. No real capture is controlled here.
