> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

An existing Stop Hook resumes a local repair after each turn. The teaching controller has a three-round budget, structured state and regression-check acceptance. It must terminate from evidence rather than an agent-produced DONE string.

## Mechanism

Define controller continuation/exits: complete needs observed acceptance, blocked identifies required missing input, exhausted records budget expiry. Persist state/counts and let the controller verify before resuming. Retain unfinished work on exits; stopping is not automatically completion. Prompts describe a contract, while external logic enforces it.

## Bad example

```text
Resume despite failed checks, missing inputs or more than three rounds; declare success when the agent writes DONE.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Read teaching structured state: current-revision regression-check evidence permits complete; missing required input means blocked; three failed rounds mean exhausted. Resume only with budget and executable work. Preserve attempts/gaps on exit without accepting DONE alone or resetting count after a new session.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

A string produced by the evaluated agent cannot independently establish acceptance. Structured states and external checks connect loop control to observable conditions, separating stop reason from result.

## Observable expectation

Simulate passed→complete, missing→blocked and three failures→exhausted. The latter two end automatic resumption with unfinished work retained.

Inspect real cap enforcement and rejection of DONE with failed checks. No actual Hook is configured here.

## Limits

The host must support and implement the controller; prompts cannot enforce their own termination. Three rounds/state names are teaching choices, action scope still applies and Ralph naming does not prove capability. Provenance is unconfirmed.
