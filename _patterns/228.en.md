> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

Plan automatic partner-data import requiring a willing/able provider of machine-readable id/status. Teaching access/sharing permission is unresolved while three button-color preferences exist. Validate direction-killing assumptions before cosmetics.

## Mechanism

Classify assumptions by failure consequences: essential direction premise, important adjustable choice and secondary optimization. Use small authorized core probes such as a permitted sample/schema check, not 'probably available'. Revise or preserve gaps when core fails; validate update model/cosmetics afterward.

## Bad example

```text
Test button colors first, assume data/access and continue full implementation even when the required feed is unavailable.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
First inspect whether the partner can/will supply an authorized machine-readable id/status sample. Record source/field/permission/delivery gaps. This is necessary for the current automatic-import direction; if unsupported pause dependent implementation and revise instead of inventing data. Then examine adjustable update cadence, then colors, staying within approved samples.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Progress on preferences does not establish core feasibility. Consequence-based ordering exposes directional failure before accumulating dependent implementation.

## Observable expectation

An illustrative table lists data/sharing as core, cadence adjustable and color secondary. Missing evidence remains unconfirmed; absent status fails this input contract.

The first probe must support/refute direction, not equate a color experiment with feasibility.

## Limits

Importance depends on objectives; manual/alternative approaches may redraw assumptions rather than prove all product value impossible. One sample does not establish scale, quality or durable supply. No real partner data is accessed.
