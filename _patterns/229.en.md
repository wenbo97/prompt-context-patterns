> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

Two workers receive authorized fixed publish-a target/payload. Teaching storage atomically enforces unique claims with a canonical payload digest. Read-absent then act permits both concurrent workers through.

## Mechanism

Use enforced atomic uniqueness before effects, binding operation/target/payload. Same key/body follows pending/replay contract; changed body fails. Persist results and reconcile unknown timeout/crash. Claim success is not external completion.

## Bad example

```text
Both see absent publish-a, publish then insert the key; reuse a changed article under it as prior success.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Atomically claim publish-a with target/canonical digest. Only the winner acts; duplicates receive pending/known results without another effect. Reject changed payloads. Record actual delivery or unknown on response loss, reconcile before releasing another sender and retain original authorization constraints.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Atomic uniqueness selects one winner rather than racing reads; payload binding prevents key reuse for another intent. Unknown outcomes prevent a local gate from masquerading as exactly-once delivery.

## Observable expectation

Same-key/body concurrency allows one send; changed article fails; lost responses retain unknown.

Inspect enforced uniqueness, not a boolean, and target identity. No article is published.

## Limits

External delivery still needs provider guarantees/reconciliation. Define canonicalization, scope and retention without collapsing legitimate intents or expiring uncertainty for convenience.
