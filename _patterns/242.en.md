> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

A legacy project has 62% coverage and no settled path to 80%. A teaching gate can first require no decline under the same measurement contract while tracking absolute improvement separately.

## Mechanism

Record baseline code/tests/tool, denominator, direction and tolerance. Use the same included scope for coverage, where higher is better. State whether the measure is enforced. Detect comparable declines; after confirmed improvement to 65%, update under the contract so it cannot fall back to 62%. Changed metric scope needs a newly justified baseline rather than copied numbers.

## Bad example

```text
Set an arbitrary 80% to keep inherited code permanently red, or call 62% sufficient final quality. Lower baselines to hide regression.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Measure and record current 62% with scope/command, require comparable nonregression and update after verified 65%. Track higher absolute quality separately and say whether the gate is wired or merely measured. Explain denominator changes before resetting the baseline.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

A measured ratchet detects deterioration instead of demanding an immediately unreachable number. Tightening retains gains without presenting inherited low quality as sufficient.

## Observable expectation

With zero regression tolerance,62→61 fails;62→65 passes and updates after confirmation, then 64 fails. Excluding files to raise the ratio is not comparable improvement. Verify CI runs the gate rather than merely documenting it.

## Limits

Nonregression does not establish sufficient quality. Coverage and complexity have different directions/denominators; noisy measures need defined tolerance. Frozen measured, provisional and enforced states differ and must be labeled accordingly.
