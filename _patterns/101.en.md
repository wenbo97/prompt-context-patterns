> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

Create a poster for a night-time astronomy lecture using fictional teaching details: 'City Stargazing', November 8 at 19:00, Science Museum second floor. You want a coherent visual direction while retaining event information, rather than an uncheckable demand for artistic quality.

## Mechanism

State a concept, then connect it to space, color, graphics and text hierarchy. This example chooses quiet observation: a dark field, one star map, empty space and a clear event-details area. Sketch the hierarchy before rendering, then inspect the concept and information in the result. These choices are teaching conventions, not a fixed recipe prescribed by the source.

## Bad example

```text
Use the event name, date and location above to make an astronomy poster. Make it beautiful, artistic, museum quality and impressive.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Use the supplied event details. First describe a quiet-observation concept and explain its implications for space, color, star imagery and text hierarchy. The teaching direction uses a dark field, one star-map motif, generous empty space and a separate date/location area. Sketch the hierarchy, then render it. Verify that City Stargazing, November 8 at 19:00 and Science Museum second floor are readable. Adjust composition if needed, explaining how it serves the concept.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

The bad instruction leaves its quality demand in adjectives. The improved version uses the concept as a reason for visual decisions and connects it to visible elements. It leaves room for interpretation while making information completeness inspectable.

## Observable expectation

Compare the sketch with the rendered result. An illustrative hierarchy emphasizes the event name, uses the star map as one visual subject and keeps date/location separate and readable. A covered location, missing title or unexplained departure from quiet observation requires revision.

Inspect legibility at the intended display size. No real poster was produced or measured here; concept consistency is not a measured aesthetic effect.

## Limits

Use a concept to coordinate creative decisions. Event facts and required readability still constrain the work. Several concepts may be valid and need audience or user judgment; naming a concept does not establish a quality improvement.
