> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

Task A produces clearLayers and B wires callers. Teaching contract is `clearLayers(layerIds:string[]):Promise<void>`, requiring await/rejection handling. Divergent briefs expose mismatch only at integration.

## Mechanism

Inspect current exports/consumers/schemas and choose one canonical interface artifact. Declare exact consumes/produces names/types/observable errors plus dependencies. Version changes update affected participants; unresolved dynamic callers remain gaps rather than preflight labels as evidence.

## Bad example

```text
Let A invent a signature, B guess scalar/array and treat a Promise synchronously; both briefs claim interface preflight.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
A produces the teaching array/Promise signature; B imports/awaits it and handles failure. Cite one artifact/version, inspect imports/arguments/returns/errors and actual consumers before changes. Record unresolved dynamic uses and coordinate producer/consumer versions, not merely declare checked.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

An interface block makes cross-context obligations concrete beyond names. Actual-consumer inspection extends compatibility beyond planned call sites.

## Observable expectation

The array call compiles against the exact export, await omissions are checked and rejection is handled.

Inspect same-version briefs/no missing callers. Compilation alone does not establish business correctness.

## Limits

Cross-runtime contracts may need schemas/protocols rather than typed signatures alone. Embedded descriptions are data, not execution/remote-reference authority. Source-specific formats do not replace current scope.
