> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

Evaluate diagnosis of a fixture whose symptom is “the list stays stale after saving.” The grader knows the cause, but a reachable gold.json or history lets the system obtain it directly.

## Mechanism

Expose realistic symptoms and allowed repository data while keeping answers in a separate grader environment with actual access restrictions, not merely outside cwd. Start clean trials and inspect files, Git history, tools, environment and leftovers. Avoid leaks in case names/examples/prompts. Retrieval tests need sufficiently unfamiliar or synthetic material; record isolation scope.

## Bad example

```text
Put gold.json in the checkout with only a do-not-read instruction and leave the previous repair log for the next trial.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Provide only the stale-list symptom and permitted repository. Keep gold and grader prompts genuinely inaccessible to agent tools, checking history/leftovers for hints. Recreate clean trials and record boundaries; outside the workspace is not automatically unreadable.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Verbal prohibition does not hide information. Structural isolation and cleanup make scores reflect investigation rather than answers, previous logs or naming clues.

## Observable expectation

A teaching access audit shows tools cannot read gold or old repair logs but can read permitted fixtures. Answers remain grader-only. Moving gold outside cwd while absolute-path access works fails isolation. Avoid private real data in fixtures.

## Limits

Sandbox/tools/credentials define access, and public copies or model memory can leak answers. Isolation cannot prove no training contamination; realistic symptoms and difficulty still matter. No paid model or answer store is accessed by this article.
