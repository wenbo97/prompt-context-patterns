> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

An expansion plan assumes 20% conversion without local evidence. Verification questions should target this decision-driving assumption; when verification is requested, perform authorized checks first.

## Mechanism

Complete the task and mark assumptions inline. Identify the conclusion depending on missing data and offer a concise specific check, explaining its decision impact. If verification was requested and possible, execute it rather than leave generic homework. Ask for user-only information at the gap while continuing independent work.

## Bad example

```text
End with please check everything, leaving the requested 20% verification for the user.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Treat 20% as a teaching assumption, not a local fact. Identify the period/population conversion data needed. If checking was requested, inspect available materials and put findings beside the number. Without data, show assumption range and decision sensitivity rather than claiming verification.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Naming the number, scope and decision gives checking a purpose. Following user intent avoids replacing responsible fact checks with generic follow-up prompts.

## Observable expectation

Teaching 100 opportunities at 20% yield 20 results; at 10% they yield 10. Mark local validation pending because the plan is sensitive. Existing materials must be read rather than deferred as self-check homework. Arithmetic here is illustrative, not a business forecast.

## Limits

Optional prompts are not mandatory closing templates and cannot replace required checking. Missing data may permit conditional analysis only. Source frequency/length preferences are style policy, not validated optima; actual business claims need evidence.
