> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

Import 120 teaching records in chosen 50-item batches verified within current limits. Two batches confirm 100; batch three has five acknowledged, five failed and ten unknown. Do not restart everything or treat unknown as not written.

## Mechanism

Keep a fixed inventory/stable identity and per-item confirmed/failed/unknown states plus continuation. Resume by reconciling checkpoint/external results: skip acknowledged work, handle known failures under contract and reconcile unknowns or use verified idempotency. Account for every input ID rather than hiding mixed results in one batch status.

## Bad example

```text
Restart all 120 after batch three, assume ten missing responses mean absence and create new records until success.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Persist 105 acknowledged, five failed and ten unknown with service/local identity mapping. Exclude acknowledged IDs on resume, handle explicit failures and reconcile unknown targets or use verified idempotent recovery. Retain checkpoints/open lists for all 120 without recreating the entire job. Fifty is this fixture's batch choice, not every service's limit.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Checkpoints preserve confirmed work while per-item outcomes retain partial success. This prevents response loss from turning uncertain existing writes into duplicates.

## Observable expectation

The teaching unresolved list contains 15 items, with ten reconciled before retry, while 105 IDs are not recreated. Counts reconcile to 120.

Inspect target identity/item evidence rather than counts only. No real leads are imported.

## Limits

Checkpointing alone does not ensure exactly-once effects; receiver uniqueness/idempotency/reconciliation is needed. Read paging differs from write recovery, and local labels do not replace service IDs. Frozen batch guidance is not a universal current limit.
