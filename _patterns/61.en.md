> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

Incident data contains public status, internal diagnostics, customer payloads and credentials for an external support summary. Define a four-level teaching matrix first; internal-tool origin grants no transfer permission.

## Mechanism

Classify fields under supplied policy: public to authorized support, internal to approved internal destinations, restricted to designated processing environments, secret to credential facilities. Declare transformations such as approved public status/nonidentifying counts. Hold unknown fields, inspect proposed payload/recipient and verify task sending authority with policy revision recorded.

## Bad example

```text
Send the whole internal-tool result externally and assume redaction makes every class public.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Apply the teaching four-level matrix, emitting only approved public status/aggregate counts. Exclude customer payloads/internal diagnostics/secrets and hold unknowns; transformation does not automatically downgrade. Check destination and existing send authority before execution.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Policy follows meaning/destination rather than tool reputation. Field-level and transformation rules prevent redaction/aggregation becoming unconditional disclosure.

## Observable expectation

A teaching payload containing customer content/secrets violates the matrix; approved status/count may remain. Unknown account labels need classification, not default public. Record target/policy without exposing originals as evidence.

## Limits

No frozen source is confirmed; this is local teaching policy, not law/provider permission. Small aggregates can identify people. Classification and sending authority are separate; matrix compliance does not itself authorize external transmission.
