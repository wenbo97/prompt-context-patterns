> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

A developer lacks a partner retention rule needed for cache cleanup. Teaching policy and infrastructure owners know permitted retention and deletion mechanism respectively; one recipient may not know both. Repeated guessing questions to the developer do not solve ownership.

## Mechanism

Establish the needed decision, knowledge owner/background and existing authoritative artifacts. Turn each genuine gap into an answerable targeted question, ordered by importance, with context and unknown/refer-to-owner options. Deliver a sendable draft; actual sending/tracking needs its own authorization.

## Bad example

```text
Question the developer until they guess 30 days, treat it as partner policy and email unidentified recipients every question.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Prepare a cache-decision questionnaire. Confirm policy/infrastructure roles and needed facts. Ask policy owners about allowed retention/basis/exceptions and infrastructure owners about deletion mechanisms/limits. Permit unknown/referral, explain how answers inform caching and avoid inducing guesses. Deliver the draft without sending it automatically.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Useful questions connect the recipient's knowledge to the requester's decision. This routes gaps to actual owners rather than producing apparent certainty through repeated questions.

## Observable expectation

An illustrative draft maps each required cache fact to a question/role and purpose. Unknown developer knowledge stays unknown without invented 30-day policy.

Check complete necessary coverage, no unauthorized guesses as rules and no unauthorized send.

## Limits

Roles do not guarantee authority; verify official material/version. Split by real owners and avoid forcing one person to answer everything. This supplies neither legal periods nor private contact data.
