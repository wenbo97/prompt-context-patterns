> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

A founder says we care about quality without an incident, then teaching context adds a customer launch delayed by missing deployment steps. Elicit grounded positioning rather than turn a vague phrase into a settled value proposition.

## Mechanism

Ask one useful probe on the current topic, briefly paraphrase then seek incident/consequence/reason. Separate statements/interpretation. Close repeated no-new-information threads with evidence and open items; respect topic changes/stops. Counts/5-Whys are heuristics, not exhaustive knowledge.

## Bad example

```text
Send twenty abstract questions, derive complete positioning from quality and continue until the user agrees despite no new evidence.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Ask for one customer incident. Paraphrase the deployment-story consequence and ask why it matters. Offer predictable delivery as a candidate interpretation, not a quote. Close under agreed saturation conditions when probes add no information, retaining evidence/inferences/open items. Respect topic changes without treating fatigue/silence as confirmation.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Focused probes deepen actual answers into inspectable events/reasons. Candidate interpretation preserves attribution, and stopping rules bound ineffective interrogation.

## Observable expectation

An illustrative note records delayed launch, provenance, predictable-delivery candidate and probes adding no information. Keep consensus/acceptance unclaimed.

Generic quality positioning or two unanswered prompts treated as saturation fails.

## Limits

Saturation is contextual to a topic/interview, not market/user completeness. Respect privacy/storage/willingness. No real interview occurs here.
