> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

Create a retry-policy quiz requiring waits and at most two retries for transient errors. A uniquely bold/long/detailed correct option lets learners answer from presentation.

## Mechanism

Establish the question’s correct-answer basis and plausible distractors. Keep length, syntax, detail and emphasis comparable without distorting accuracy for equal counts. Vary positions with mappings recorded. Hide explanations/answer-bearing helper text until choice, then give policy-based feedback. Inspect the complete presentation for format-only shortcuts.

## Bad example

```text
Make the correct answer a long bold paragraph while others say immediately or ignore, encouraging the longest-option guess.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Offer comparably detailed bounded-wait, immediate-bounded and ongoing-wait retry options. Express each clearly without unique emphasis; the prompt determines correctness. Explain waits/two-retry limit after choice, without making distractors vague to force identical lengths.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Comparable presentation reduces association with irrelevant format. Reasoned feedback also exposes poor distractors instead of merely revealing a letter.

## Observable expectation

Check unique length/boldness/fixed placement. If correctness remains guessable with content hidden, revise presentation. Wrong-choice feedback cites violated requirements without changing the key. This is not empirical quiz validity.

## Limits

Equal word counts cannot remove semantic clues or establish validity. Frozen exact-count advice is a strategy, with clarity/accuracy taking priority. Avoid teaching false rules through distractors; learning outcomes need validation.
