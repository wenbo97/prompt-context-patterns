> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

A coverage gate requires 70% and the candidate has 68%. A patch lowers it to 60% and skips a failing test, then appears green. Review both implementation and changes to the success bar.

## Mechanism

Fix scope from branch baseline through the full tree, including staged, unstaged and new files. Compare thresholds, skipped/deleted tests, removed assertions, suppressions, stubs and exceptions; inspect meaning to distinguish legitimate replacement from weakening. Meet the original contract or record exception reason, scope, owner and expiry for authority-based ruling. An unavailable guard means unknown, not clean.

## Bad example

```text
Lower 70% to 60%, skip the failure and report the fix complete.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Compare the full patch with the original 70% bar. Report lowering and skip explicitly, repair uncovered behavior first, or propose a scoped, explained, expiring exception for ruling. Green cannot hide a lower bar, and text matches cannot alone reject equivalent test rewrites.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

When authors change checks too, green can come from weaker conditions. Reviewing the bar preserves the original meaning of success instead of substituting a pass label for behavior.

## Observable expectation

Teaching 68% fails 70% and passes 60%, but the lowering is a finding. Removing redundant tests with preserved equivalent coverage needs semantic review. Missing baseline or a guard error makes checking incomplete.

## Limits

Shallow diff guards can misflag refactors and miss semantic weakening. External tools also have limits/configuration. Standards can be revised with evidence and authority, not silently bypassed. Nondeterministic metrics require comparable conditions.
