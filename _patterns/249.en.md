> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

A cache allows up to 30 seconds of staleness. Review says any stale data is wrong, while another path really permits unbounded staleness. Neither fixing nor dismissing both wholesale is appropriate.

## Mechanism

Reread artifact and supplied contract per finding. Classify in order: repair missing/misread contract first; fix valid actionable defects; record accepted trade-offs with scope/reasons; explain context-based noise. Retain evidence, ruling and next action, then reclassify after contract clarification. Authorized owners accept trade-offs, not an author’s unsupported closure.

## Bad example

```text
Make every request synchronous because review objects to staleness, or ignore unlimited staleness because some staleness is allowed.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Clarify the 30-second tolerance, inspect both paths and reassess compliant bounded behavior. Treat unbounded staleness as actionable. Record accepted latency trade-offs separately with evidence/scope and retain unresolved items. Repair based on facts rather than finding count.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Review output is judgment data; artifact reconciliation avoids rubber-stamping. Contract-first classification reduces unnecessary edits while preserving genuine failure paths.

## Observable expectation

Teaching 20-second staleness is within contract;45 seconds or unbounded behavior needs violation checking. If the reviewer lacked the tolerance, record clarification/review rather than dismissal. Every item links evidence and ruling.

## Limits

Classification requires judgment; disagreement proves neither side right. Hidden needs and unapproved trade-offs may require an owner. Do not retroactively write a contract to authorize behavior. Frozen precedence is a method, not project authority.
