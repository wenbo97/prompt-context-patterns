> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

Investigate which skill influenced last month’s session. Teaching materials include a captured body/digest and today’s HEAD. Current checkout cannot authenticate a past installation, and missing original paths do not erase preserved copies.

## Mechanism

Scope claims by time and label historical evidence, unverified snapshot, current observation or unknown. Use captures/archives for past claims and present checkout for now; record retrieved_at separately from as_of. Verify copy provenance/session binding and leave uncaptured installation versions unknown. Cached data obtained today remains uncertain if observation time is unknown.

## Bad example

```text
Use today’s HEAD as last month’s version and call a retrieved old cache current verified state.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Use captured body/hash for that historical file, label today’s HEAD separately and leave whole-install version unknown. Keep retrieval and observation times distinct and verify archive provenance without generalizing one file to the environment.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Time forms part of evidence meaning. Separating acquisition from observation prevents old information becoming current by relabeling, and file evidence authenticates only its object.

## Observable expectation

Teaching records name session date, captured body, present checkout and unknown installation fields. Empty as_of with today’s retrieved_at cannot automatically become fresh. Inspect archives despite lost paths; unknown origin remains unverified.

## Limits

Timestamps/digests do not establish truth or environment-wide identity. Cache, local patches and timezones can differ. Limit historical conclusions rather than inventing the past from present state.
