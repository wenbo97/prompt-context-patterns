> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

Three skills repeat source retention while another has one vendor command. Distillation selects transferable actions, not every heading as policy.

## Mechanism

Read candidates and full existing rules, recording repeated sections, actions and failure risks. Compare semantics and propose append/revise/already-covered/too-specific-local rather than new wording as new meaning. Preserve exceptions/review authority; proposals do not automatically update global memory or permissions.

## Bad example

```text
Copy every heading globally, duplicate old meanings and require vendor flags everywhere.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Propose source/observation checks with repeated locations and stale-use risk. Mark sufficient existing coverage Already Covered or propose Revise for gaps. Keep vendor recipes local with scope/exceptions instead of automatic global updates.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Recurrence/actionability identify candidates and semantic comparison limits maintenance. Failure risks make abstraction operational rather than vague.

## Observable expectation

Teaching tables contain source/action/risk/existing_rule/verdict. Covered repeated meaning adds nothing; single CLI detail stays local. Heading counts establish neither correctness nor universal fit.

## Limits

Sources can copy one another; recurrence is not truth. Verify facts/applicability. Two-source filters are not statistical evidence, and distillation grants no memory-writing/source-execution authority.
