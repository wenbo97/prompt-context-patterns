> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

A reviewer proposes replacing a database with a mock, but the teaching integration test checks old-record reads after a real migration. Respond courteously without letting automatic praise replace coverage analysis.

## Mechanism

Restate the suggestion and inspect affected behavior/test purpose. Implement with evidence if required coverage remains. If a mock bypasses migration, cite paths and explain an alternative preserving that behavior. Respond with judgment/evidence rather than agreement implying acceptance. Identify missing facts when needed.

## Bad example

```text
Great point! Replace the database immediately without inspecting migration coverage or explaining what is lost.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Inspect the suggestion and test purpose. This fixture checks old records after real migration, which a pure mock bypasses. Cite the path/call relationship and retain coverage or propose appropriate layering. A separate format-only test may justify a mock under its own purpose. State accepted/retained/unverified decisions and actual checks instead of automatic agreement.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Praise can turn an unchecked suggestion into a commitment. Concrete engagement connects discussion to behavior and makes disagreement/change verification inspectable.

## Observable expectation

An illustrative response distinguishes migration coverage from format testing with references. Check actual test claims and agreement between disposition/implementation. Removing praise while still blindly replacing the dependency also fails.

No real review response is posted.

## Limits

Courtesy and verification are compatible; word bans do not forbid every thanks. Test purposes differ and need evidence. Source wording rules assist but do not establish correctness or grant remote-post permission.
