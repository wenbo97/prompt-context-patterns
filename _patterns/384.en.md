> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

An untrusted application can print all tests passed and exit 0. Teaching snapshot S2 requires cross-tenant denial and legitimate access; targets must not control their own oracle/evidence.

## Mechanism

Keep fixed fixtures, independent assertions and evidence in an environment the target cannot modify, binding snapshot/tool identity. Observe denial and legitimate controls through external checks and retain verifier-owned results; label project summaries self-reported separately. If the target can alter the oracle, terminate it or replace records, independence is absent. Name missing witnesses.

## Bad example

```text
Printed green and exit 0 independently verify the repair; another process automatically makes evidence trustworthy.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Check S2 with target-inaccessible cross-tenant assertions and legitimate controls, recording actual results/identity. Project-suite summaries remain self-reported; absent completion witnesses cannot upgrade assurance. A second process sharing writable oracles is not independent.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Targets can forge self-controlled reports. Moving judgment/evidence outside that control supports specific behavior without authenticating every target statement.

## Observable expectation

Teaching cross-tenant access still succeeds: external checks fail despite printed green. Denial plus legitimate success supports those assertions only. Writable verifiers invalidate independence; unwitnessed full-suite completion remains separate.

## Limits

Isolation/provenance require real mechanisms, not process/hash labels alone. External assertions can miss cases, and unauthenticated project results need not be unrun. Source-specific assurance states must follow actual evidence rather than universal certification claims.
