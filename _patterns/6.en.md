> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

You need an incident recommendation from configuration evidence. config/service.yml line 4 contains `timeout_ms: 500`. The incident record reports a timeout but supplies neither a latency distribution nor a record of the configuration actually loaded. Avoid generic advice and an unsupported increase of the timeout.

## Mechanism

Name the observed failure in the instruction, then supply an allowed replacement. Here, prohibit configuration advice without a parameter and evidence. The replacement cites the known value, lists missing evidence and identifies the next read. Unknown values stay unknown.

Each prohibition should address a concrete failure in this task and explain what to do instead.

## Bad example

```text
Write an incident recommendation from config/service.yml and the incident record above. Make it useful and careful. Do not make mistakes.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Write an incident recommendation from the configuration and incident record above. Do not merely say 'check the configuration', and do not recommend increasing a timeout without evidence. Cite the parameter, observed value and material location. If latency or loaded-configuration records are missing, list those gaps and name the next record to read and what to inspect. Do not invent a replacement value.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Useful and careful do not specify how to recognize an acceptable recommendation. The improved instruction makes the prohibition observable and supplies a fallback for missing evidence. The agent need not choose between vague advice and an invented fix.

## Observable expectation

An illustrative recommendation identifies timeout_ms=500 at config/service.yml:4, states that the loaded value and latency distribution are unknown, and asks to inspect the startup configuration record and latency for the incident window.

Check for a real location, both missing inputs and no invented timeout. A list of prohibited phrases without the next inspection step is still incomplete.

## Limits

Use bans for identifiable failure modes rather than building an exhaustive list that hides the task. The located source pairs forbidden responses with alternatives; this incident fixture and inspection sequence are independent teaching constructions. They do not establish that changing a timeout will resolve the incident.
