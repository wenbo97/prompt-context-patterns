> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

A host accepts numeric shapes but not teaching price>=0/positive-integer qty. The wire projection provides types only; strict labels do not enforce removed business constraints.

## Mechanism

Retain the canonical full contract/validator, derive verified supported projection and inventory omissions. Check full validation plus parsing/refusal/truncation before use. Descriptions and host labels replace no actual enforcement.

## Bad example

```text
Drop minimum, accept price=-5 and claim complete policy because strict.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Use supported wire types while retaining full teaching validation for nonnegative price/positive-integer qty. Map omissions to real checks and reject invalid/incomplete/refused/truncated output before quote creation. Schema compilation is not business validation.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Compatibility governs transport while the full validator governs permissible data. Separate layers preserve policy through adaptation.

## Observable expectation

Price=-5/qty2 may fit numeric wire types but fails full validation; price 0/qty1 satisfies these numeric rules. Inspect every omitted condition's enforcement and incomplete-data rejection.

Passing establishes no source truth; no quote is created.

## Limits

Host/SDK subsets change, and validators need tests. Frozen support lists are not current guarantees or authorization/truth evidence.
