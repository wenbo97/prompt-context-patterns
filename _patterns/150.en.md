> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

A user approved three local catalog tasks: repair links, generate index, verify output; publication is outside scope. Completing one task should advance to the next without another continue question. Preserve goal/interrupted step during side questions.

## Mechanism

Read approved objectives/boundaries, carry out necessary steps and record evidence. Continue across task boundaries; wait only where a required decision/new scope affects dependent work, continuing independent work. Answer side status questions briefly then resume. Honor explicit cancellation, pause or direction changes.

## Bad example

```text
Ask whether to continue after every link, lose the main task after a side answer, or interpret continuous execution as permission to publish.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
Complete authorized local repair→generation→verification with recorded evidence, without reauthorizing each step. Briefly answer status questions then return to the interrupted step. Publication remains outside scope. Explain missing required decisions and wait for dependent actions while independent work proceeds. Pause/cancel requests prevail; silence/defaults are not approval.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Task completion is not automatically a new permission boundary. Retaining scope and interrupted position preserves continuity without overreach or goal loss.

## Observable expectation

The teaching record covers all three local tasks/checks with no repeated continue requests or publication. Side answers resume work; explicit pause stops it.

Failed verification remains a gap rather than completion merely because every task was touched.

## Limits

Continuity does not permit unlimited auto-decisions. New irreversible effects, access scope or critical intent gaps still need the relevant decision. Current host/user constraints govern, not source-specific auto-adjudication rules.
