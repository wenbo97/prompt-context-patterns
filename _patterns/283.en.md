> These examples and illustrative results are independently authored teaching materials, not measured model results.

## Use case

One session’s cumulative token observations are 100,140,200. Usage between first and last is not their sum 440. A separate per-call total task has different boundaries.

## Mechanism

Establish incremental/cumulative semantics, units, session/call identity and starting boundary. Sum non-overlapping increments; difference cumulative endpoints and segment resets. For call totals, take one valid final snapshot per non-overlapping call. Preserve missing values and establish parent/child inclusion before aggregating. Tokens, characters, costs and cache categories are distinct.

## Bad example

```text
Sum 100+140+200 as 440 for the interval, fill missing samples with 0 and treat output_chars as tokens.
```
{: .example--bad}

## Good example

```text
These are cumulative observations of one counter: the 100-to 200 interval uses 100. Supported intermediate deltas are 40 and 60; without the middle sample, report the interval only. For call totals, count final snapshots once and check resets/nested duplication.
```
{: .example--good}

## Why the change matters

Cumulative samples include earlier usage. Semantics/boundaries determine aggregation, while identity and categories prevent sampled/nested/different-unit double counting.

## Observable expectation

Teaching 200−100=100, with 40+60. A fall 200→10 may be reset, not negative 190 or automatically 10 for the interval. Repeated final 200 counts once; missing baseline means unknown interval.

## Limits

Usage/cache fields differ across providers and require actual contracts. Characters are not measured tokens, and tokens are not currency. Billing also needs model, pricing basis and failed attempts; frozen rates are not current prices.
