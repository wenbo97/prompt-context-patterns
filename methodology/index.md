# Editorial criteria and provenance

This reference separates three questions: is a method useful for a concrete task, where is its source instance, and has its effect been measured?

## Admission criteria

Each criterion scores 0 for absent, 1 for partial or abstract, and 2 for specific and complete. Admission requires at least eight of ten, with the first three each scoring two.

| Criterion | What the review checks |
| --- | --- |
| Scenario | A concrete task and known conditions |
| Mechanism | An instruction or operation the reader can apply |
| Contrast | Bad and good examples for the same task and facts |
| Observable result | A result or artifact the reader can inspect |
| Boundaries | Conditions where the method needs adaptation or should stop |

These are editorial criteria, not measured success probabilities. Duplicate mechanisms merge; weak or incorrect methods are revised and reassessed, or withdrawn. Old identities remain reachable through migration pages.

## Source states

A located source has a repository, immutable commit, file, section and line range. It proves an observed instance. It does not establish the first inventor. Earliest provenance is recorded separately.

**Source unconfirmed** means the original text has not been located. A method can retain this label while meeting the same usefulness criteria. Finding a source does not excuse bad advice: examples and factual claims are reviewed independently.

## Teaching examples and experiments

Every pattern shows independently constructed teaching examples. They are not historical model outputs. Actual experiments identify their fixtures, model/configuration, run count and available outputs. Historical reports without raw outputs are labelled accordingly, and their numerical claims are not promoted to current evidence.
